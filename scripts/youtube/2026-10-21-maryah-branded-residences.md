## CHAPTER 2: What a Branded Residence Actually Is (3:30–5:30)
*[Visual: You walking through a luxury lobby (film with permission) or a high-end B-roll stand-in. Simple animated diagram.]*

"A branded residence is a home you own outright, built and sold with a hotel or luxury brand's name, design standards and, usually, its services.

Three parties are involved:

*[On-screen diagram: DEVELOPER builds it → BRAND licenses its name and standards → OPERATOR runs the services]*

The developer builds and sells. The brand lends its name and sets the standards. And an operator, often the hotel itself, runs the services: concierge, housekeeping, valet, maybe access to the hotel's spa, pool and restaurants.

You're not buying a hotel room. You own the apartment. You're paying for the experience around it, and for the brand's promise that the building will be run to a certain standard for years.

That promise is the product. Remember that, because it's also where the risk is. We'll come back to it."

## CHAPTER 4: The Numbers. What Is the Premium? (7:30–10:00)
*[Visual: Clean data graphics. You on camera between graphics.]*

"Globally, branded homes sell for about 44% more than comparable non-branded homes. [VERIFY: Brand Standard / GBR 2026]

In Abu Dhabi, CBRE has estimated that premium at around 87%. [VERIFY: CBRE 2025] That's more than double the global average.

*[On-screen bar chart: Global ~44% · Dubai ~56% (H1 2026) · Abu Dhabi ~87% (CBRE, 2025)]*

On Al Maryah specifically, one transaction analysis puts the median branded home at around 3,700 dirhams per square foot, against about 1,850 for the island overall. [VERIFY: EstateIQ, single source] Some of the top branded towers trade well above 4,000 a square foot.

*[Pattern interrupt: you lean in to camera]*

Now, here's the part people skip. A big premium isn't automatically bad. It's only bad if it isn't justified, or if it can't hold.

There are three reasons people pay it:

**One: the service.** For international owners who live here part of the year, lock-and-leave with someone running everything is worth real money.

**Two: resale.** A recognised brand can be easier to sell to the next buyer, especially to someone abroad who's never seen the building. The brand does the trust-building for you.

**Three: scarcity and status.** There are only so many Four Seasons residences in Abu Dhabi. For some buyers, that's the whole point.

*[Re-hook]* But premiums can shrink. And on this island, there's one big reason they might."

## CHAPTER 5: The Risks Nobody Puts in the Brochure (10:00–12:30)
*[Visual: You on camera, more serious tone. Simple text cards for each risk.]*

"This is the part I'd want to hear if I were the buyer.

**Risk one: supply.** Abu Dhabi has 26 branded projects in the pipeline. [VERIFY] When branded becomes common, it stops being special, and the premium can compress. The question isn't 'is it branded?' It's 'will this brand still be rare when I sell?'

**Risk two: service charges.** All those services cost money every year. Branded towers usually carry noticeably higher service charges than standard ones. Get the actual figure per square foot before you decide, and work out what it does to your net yield.

*[On-screen: Ask for the service charge budget, not an estimate]*

**Risk three: the brand agreement.** The brand's name is there under a management or licence agreement, and agreements have terms. Ask how long it runs, and what happens to the building, and your value, if it ends.

**Risk four: rental rules.** Some branded buildings restrict short-term rentals, or require you to use their rental programme with their fee. That changes your return. Read the rules before you buy.

**Risk five: off-plan timing.** Some of the brands here are still being built. Handover dates can move. If it's off-plan, know exactly what the payment plan looks like if the handover slips.

None of these are deal-breakers. They're just the questions that separate a smart purchase from an expensive one."

## CHAPTER 6: My Checklist (12:30–14:00)
*[Visual: Clean numbered list building on screen. This is the screenshot moment.]*

"So here's the checklist I go through with clients before any branded purchase. Screenshot this.

*[On-screen: RAYAN'S BRANDED RESIDENCE CHECKLIST]*

1. **What exactly does the brand deliver?** List the actual services, not the marketing words.
2. **What's the service charge, and what's my net yield after it?**
3. **How long is the brand agreement, and what happens if it ends?**
4. **What are the rental rules?** Short-term allowed? Mandatory programme? Fees?
5. **Is the premium in line with resale prices in completed branded towers?** Compare to real transactions, not launch prices.
6. **Who's the developer, and what have they delivered before?**

If a project passes all six, the premium is probably buying you something real."

## CHAPTER 7: Verdict. Who Should Buy, and Who Shouldn't (14:00–14:50)
*[Visual: You walking along the Al Maryah promenade at sunset.]*

"So, is the brand worth it?

**It makes sense** if you'll live in it, even part-time, and value the service. If you're an international buyer who wants something easy to own from abroad. Or if you're buying one of the genuinely scarce, established names, and holding long-term.

**It probably doesn't** if you're chasing pure rental yield. You'll usually get more return per dirham in a well-chosen standard building on Al Reem or Yas, which I covered in my island series.

The brand isn't the investment. The building, the agreement and the price you pay are the investment. The brand is just the wrapper."
