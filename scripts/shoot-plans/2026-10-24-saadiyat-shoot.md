# Shoot Plan: Saadiyat Island · Saturday 24 Oct 2026

> Atlas · prepared 2026-10-23
> **Goal for the day:** film all of Reel 2 (Saadiyat), plus the talking-head parts of Reels 1 and 7, plus extra B-roll for later.
> Use the filming cards in `scripts/reels/2026-10-23-islands-filming-cards.md`.

## Timing (this is the most important part)
- **Sunrise ~6:24 · Sunset ~5:49 pm** (calculated times, may be off by a minute or two)
- **Best window: 3:45 pm → 6:05 pm.** Golden hour from ~4:45, then blue hour after sunset. Warm, soft light makes everything look premium. This is the Iman Gadzhi look.
- **Avoid 10 am – 3 pm.** Harsh overhead light means squinting, hard shadows and sweat.
- If you're at the site in the morning, use that time for the site visit, renders, the show villa and wide establishing shots. Save your on-camera pieces for late afternoon.

## Locations, in shooting order

### 1. The project site you're visiting (morning or whenever you're there)
- **What to get:** site hoarding wide shot, construction in progress, the sales gallery, the scale model, renders on screen, the show villa interior (slow walk-through on gimbal).
- **Ask first:** get permission from the sales or site team before filming. Construction sites usually need PPE and an escort.
- ⚠️ **[COMPLIANCE CHECK]** If the project name, logo or prices are visible, that counts as advertising a specific project. Either confirm whether you need a permit or keep the shots generic: textures, cranes, interiors without branding.
- These shots are B-roll for Reel 2 ("supply is tight"), and later for project-specific content.

### 2. Mamsha Al Saadiyat beachfront promenade (start ~3:45 pm)
- **Why:** beachfront promenade with residential buildings, cafes and the Gulf. It's the best "lifestyle on Saadiyat" backdrop and it's public.
- **Shots:**
  - Walk-and-talk along the promenade for your on-camera lines (Reel 2 hook and ending).
  - Slow pan from the sea across to the residences.
  - Coffee or café detail shots: hands, cup, sea behind. Small lifestyle moments.
- Look for an angle toward the Louvre dome from this area. If you can get the dome in the background of a talking-head shot, that's your strongest frame of the day.

### 3. Louvre Abu Dhabi, exterior only (~4:45 pm, golden hour)
- **Why:** the most recognisable image on Saadiyat. Your "cultural district" beat.
- **Shots:** dome silhouette against the sky, light reflecting on the water, slow push-in toward the dome.
- ⚠️ **Rules:** Louvre Abu Dhabi says only handheld cameras are allowed, no tripods, and commercial filming (including drones) in and around the museum is prohibited without written approval. Your content promotes your business, so keep it low-key: phone only, no tripod, no crew. Shooting from public areas outside the museum grounds is safest. Don't do a staged "shoot" on museum property.

### 4. Guggenheim Abu Dhabi construction, from a public road
- **Why:** this is your "opens 11 December" visual, and it's very timely.
- **Shots:** wide of the structure from outside the site boundary. Don't enter, and don't fly a drone.
- Back-up if access is awkward: use the official Guggenheim render (credited) in the edit instead.

### 5. Saadiyat Public Beach (~5:30 pm, sunset)
- **Why:** pristine sand and sea at sunset is pure premium mood. It works as the closing shot of Reel 2 and for the Reel 1 / Reel 7 talking heads.
- **Shots:** you walking toward the water (back to camera, then turning), feet in sand, sun setting.
- **Talking heads:** stand side-on to the sunset so the light is on your face, not behind you.

### 6. Driving shot: arriving on Saadiyat (whenever you drive in)
- A passenger films out the windscreen or side window crossing onto the island, with the skyline and road signs for "Saadiyat". This is a great opener for the series.
- Safety first: only a passenger films. Never film while driving.

## What NOT to do
- **No drone** unless it's registered with the GCAA and you're inside a green zone in the My Drone Hub app. Drones are banned around the Louvre without approval. Easier option: skip the drone tomorrow and use licensed stock footage or hire a licensed drone operator later.
- **Don't film people's faces** without consent. Filming people in public without permission is against UAE law. Frame around people or keep them out of focus.
- **Don't film inside gated villa communities** without permission from the community or developer.

## Kit checklist
- Phone set to **4K, 24 or 30 fps**. Clean the lens.
- **Gimbal** for walk-throughs and push-ins
- **Wireless lav mic. This is essential.** Beach wind ruins audio. Use a furry windscreen.
- Small light reflector or white card (optional) to fill your face at golden hour
- Power bank, water, hand towel (heat), sunglasses (remove them on camera)
- **Outfit:** light linen or a light-coloured suit, open collar, no busy patterns. Premium and relaxed. Bring a second outfit, so Reels 1, 2 and 7 don't look filmed on the same day.

## Permits: the honest picture
- Since **February 2026**, individuals publishing advertising or promotional content online in the UAE need an **advertiser permit** from the UAE Media Council. If you don't already have one, check whether it applies to your posts before publishing. [VERIFY]
- Abu Dhabi's Media Regulatory Office issues **ground filming permits** for public locations (about AED 500/day, up to 15 working days to process). It's unclear whether a solo creator filming on a phone needs one. Low-key phone filming is common, but if anyone in authority asks you to stop, stop politely. Call the MRO (800 552 255) for a definitive answer before bigger shoots. [VERIFY]
